Epistemological Foundations

The Islamic Paradigm of Technology

ثلاثية فقه التقنية: التسخير، الاستخلاف، والعلم النافع

01. TASKHEER (تَسْخِير)
Cosmic Subjection & Intelligibility

The cosmos is not a hostile wilderness to be conquered, nor an enchanted deity to be worshipped. Physical laws, water dynamics, iron metallurgy (Surah Al-Hadid), and celestial orbits are deliberately subjected by God for human flourishing and study.

02. KHILAFAH (خِلَافَة)
Moral Stewardship & Anti-Fasad

Human agency is fiduciary trusteeship (*Amanah*). The engineer does not possess absolute sovereignty over nature. Technology must never cause corruption (*Fasad fi al-Ard*), environmental exploitation, or the destruction of human dignity.

03. 'ILM NAFI' (عِلْم نَافِع)
Beneficial Utility & Ethical Purpose

Innovation is classified by its fruits. Islamic civilization prioritized technologies serving communal obligations (*Fard Kifayah*): civic water supply, medical diagnosis, astronomical prayer reckoning (*Miqat*), and knowledge democratization.

Comparative Critique

Secular Techno-Optimism vs. Islamic Realism

Analytical Dimension Western Secular Techno-Optimism Islamic Technological Paradigm
Cosmological Vision Promethean: Man conquers an indifferent universe; technology will overcome death and suffering. Theocentric: Nature is a divine trust (*Amanah*); technology is a tool to fulfill moral stewardship (*'Imarah*).
The Problem of Evil Believes social conflict and war are merely "communication/engineering glitches" that machines will fix. Recognizes moral failure stems from human spiritual disease (*Qalb*), not deficient machinery.
Ethical Framework Utilitarian accelerationism; market efficiency; "move fast and break things". Maqasid al-Shariah: Preserving Faith, Life, Intellect, Lineage, and Wealth against corruption.
View on AI & Transhumanism Strives for artificial godhood, mind uploading, and transcending the biological human condition. Rejects machine consciousness (*Rūḥ*); AI is an accountable statistical tool bounded by justice and human dignity.

About The Islamic Techno-Optimism Archive

This archive is a non-profit, open-access Digital Humanities research initiative. It systematically documents historical moments across Islamic civilization when new inventions sparked transformative civic, intellectual, and religious hopes.

By examining primary source treatises (Banu Musa, Ibn al-Haytham, Al-Jazari, Ibn al-Shatir, Taqi al-Din, and contemporary Fiqh academies), we investigate both the breathtaking mechanical genius of the tradition and the sober juristic inquiries that safeguarded societal cohesion.
